## Incorrect newer-Pingo experiment

1. The first construction of `tv-port` mistakenly combined VDP 2.15 with the
   latest `fededevi/pingo` internals while adapting TurboVega's wire protocol
   around them.
2. The strict fixtures initially cleared the output bitmap to light blue but
   rendered no geometry. They exited cleanly, and Jukebox still worked.
3. Source comparison found that newer Pingo treats `Renderer.camera_view` as a
   camera pose and inverts it inside object rendering. TurboVega's bridge
   supplies the already-final view transform. With the fixture's Z translation
   of `-25`, the extra inversion placed all geometry behind the camera.
4. A compatibility inversion made the geometry visible, proving that diagnosis,
   but the models appeared much farther away and object rotation behaved as
   though it orbited the scene origin.
5. Those additional failures demonstrated broader transform-semantic drift.
   Patching newer Pingo to imitate TurboVega would not produce a trustworthy
   historical baseline, so the experiment was abandoned rather than extended.

## Hardware observations at this baseline

1. The perspective texture-mapping defect persists, as expected. It has not
   been fixed.
2. The cube fixture sends a camera-translation command of `(0, 0, -25)` in
   1/128-unit fixed point. TurboVega places the resulting matrix directly in
   `renderer.camera_view`, so it transforms the world origin to view-space
   Z = -25. The command value must not be mistaken for the camera's world-space
   position.
3. Under the conventional inverse-view interpretation, that matrix corresponds
   to a camera at world Z = +25 looking toward the origin along negative Z. One
   would consequently expect the cube's `+Z` face to face the camera, yet the
   visible face is labeled `-Z` and reads upright. This discrepancy is evidence
   within the orientation/geometry/UV problem, not a settled handedness
   convention.
4. Rotating the cube to expose its top shows `+X`; the expected face is `+Y`.
5. The bottom shows `-X`; the expected face is `-Y`.
6. The screen-right side shows `-Y`; the expected face is `+X`.
7. The screen-left side shows `+Y`; the expected face is `-X`.
8. Translation keys otherwise move the object as expected.
9. Rotation controls otherwise respond in the expected directions.
10. Page Up moves the model downward and Page Down moves it upward. This is the
    characteristic symptom of the model being rendered upside down.
11. The upright `-Z` face label initially appears to conflict with the
    upside-down geometry symptom. That observation is intentionally preserved:
    geometry orientation, UV orientation, texture image row order, and screen
    coordinates may contain compensating errors and must be tested separately.

## Planned change 1: make VDU camera transforms describe a pose

This is the proposed implementation map, recorded before changing the VDP
source. The change is deliberately restricted to camera semantics so that its
effect remains attributable.

1. Keep the newly corrected strict fixtures at camera translation
   `(0, 0, +25)`. Under the adopted convention this means that the camera is at
   world Z = +25 and faces forward along -Z toward the cube at the origin.

2. Change the Pingo VDU bridge in `video/pingo_3d.h` from passing the camera's
   pose matrix directly to the renderer:

   ```diff
   -        renderer.camera_view = m_camera.m_transform;
   +        renderer.camera_view = p3d::mat4Inverse(&m_camera.m_transform);
   ```

   The VDU-facing camera translation and rotation commands will consequently
   have the same pose semantics as the corresponding object commands. The
   renderer will still receive the world-to-view matrix it expects.

3. Perform the pose-to-view inversion exactly once, at this bridge boundary.
   Do not also invert the camera in the renderer, fixture, or command parser.

4. Add or extend a focused host-side regression check proving that a camera
   pose at `(0, 0, +25)` produces a view transform that places the world origin
   at view-space `(0, 0, -25)`. This guards the command meaning independently
   of the still-faulty visual pipeline.

5. Run the native Pingo smoke test, the strict TurboVega protocol-scope check,
   and a full PlatformIO firmware build. These checks must pass without adding
   commands beyond TurboVega's final `0` through `40` command surface.

6. Refresh only the frozen VDP module in
   `emulators/tv-port-baseline`, retaining its isolated SD card and current
   strict cube fixture. Do not modify or replace the older, extensively
   enhanced emulator profile at `emulator`.

7. Launch the baseline emulator for human visual verification. The immediate
   expected result is that the cube becomes visible again with the corrected
   fixture value of camera Z = +25.

8. Expect the cube initially to retain TurboVega's other known defects:
   upside-down screen-space presentation, incorrect face/orientation results,
   and non-perspective-correct texture interpolation. Their persistence is
   useful evidence that this change corrected only camera pose semantics.

9. If the cube does not return, stop and diagnose this isolated change rather
   than compensating elsewhere in the pipeline.

10. Do not include the screen-space Y inversion, texture-row convention,
    perspective-correct UV interpolation, projection changes, object-transform
    changes, or newer-upstream Pingo behavior in this implementation. Those
    remain separate attributable changes.

## Planned change 2: flip viewport Y exactly once

This experiment is being performed with Codex set to its lowest concentration
level, “Light.” The result is being recorded so the Author can compare the
quality and token cost of different reasoning-effort settings.

1. Preserve backface culling exactly as written. It operates on normalized
   device coordinates before the viewport conversion, where `+Y` correctly
   points up, so the target-memory Y reflection must not change its sign.

2. Convert normalized-device Y to the top-origin render target with one
   explicit reflection:

   ```diff
   -a.y * halfY + halfY
   +-a.y * halfY + halfY
   ```

   Apply the same expression to all three projected vertices.

3. The reflection reverses the sign of screen-space triangle area and all
   three barycentric edge weights. Replace the rasterizer's assumption that
   inside weights are always nonnegative with a test that accepts weights
   having the same sign as the computed area.

4. Do not change model matrices, rotation matrices, camera transforms,
   projection, NDC culling, texture coordinates, texture-row direction, or
   perspective interpolation.

5. Build the native VDP module and place it only in the isolated baseline
   emulator for a provisional test. The Author must validate the emulator
   before any related commit, push, hardware build, or flash.

6. Acceptance requires X, Y, and Z translations and rotations all to appear in
   the agreed directions. The cube must retain its expected visible geometry;

## Planned change 3: repair UV mapping

This plan is being drafted with Codex set to its lowest concentration level,
“Light.” UV orientation and perspective interpolation are separate faults and
will be corrected as separately attributable, hardware-qualified stages.

### Stage 3A: correct texture row orientation

1. Preserve the now-qualified camera, object transforms, projection, viewport
   Y reflection, NDC culling, and rasterizer edge-sign handling.

2. Treat fixture UVs as Blender/OBJ coordinates: `u = 0` is the left texture
   edge and `v = 0` is the bottom edge. Treat raw RGBA texture memory as
   top-row first.

3. Confirm several known cube UV coordinates against identifiable pixels in
   `blenderaxes.rgba8` before editing. This supplies a non-rendered reference
   for the expected top, bottom, left, and right samples.

4. Perform the bottom-origin UV to top-origin image-row conversion exactly
   once in `texture_readF()`, conceptually:

   ```c
   image_y = (1.0f - v) * (height - 1);
   ```

   Do not flip V in fixture generation, VDU decoding, object storage,
   interpolation, geometry, or viewport conversion.

5. Correct the independent rectangular-texture indexing defect in the same
   sampler: Y must be bounded or wrapped by `f->size.y`, never
   `f->size.x`. Define endpoint behavior deliberately so `u` or `v` at exactly
   `1.0` cannot read beyond the final row or column.

6. Do not change the existing perspective interpolation formula during this
   orientation stage. Its distortion must remain visible so row orientation
   can be accepted independently.

7. Build the firmware and test the strict cube on real hardware first. The
   cube's labels and decorations must read vertically upright while all six
   already-qualified translation and rotation directions remain correct.

8. After hardware passes, build and launch the isolated baseline emulator to
   confirm behavioral agreement. Leave all emulator-related changes
   uncommitted and unpushed until the Author explicitly validates them.

9. Commit Stage 3A only after both hardware and required emulator validation.
   Record the exact sampler rule and fixture observations in the comparison
   document.

### Stage 3B: implement perspective-correct UV interpolation

10. Preserve the hardware-qualified Stage 3A sampler orientation. Do not use
    texture flipping, altered fixture UVs, or geometry changes to conceal
    interpolation errors.

11. Add a host-side numerical check for one triangle with unequal clip-space
    W values. Compute the expected UV at selected interior pixels from
    barycentric interpolation of `u/w`, `v/w`, and `1/w`.

12. Retain each projected vertex's reciprocal clip-space W before perspective
    division. Do not substitute NDC Z or the later depth value for W.

13. For every vertex, prepare `u_over_w`, `v_over_w`, and `one_over_w`.
    Interpolate those three quantities with the existing screen-space
    barycentric weights, then reconstruct:

    ```text
    u = interpolated(u_over_w) / interpolated(one_over_w)
    v = interpolated(v_over_w) / interpolated(one_over_w)
    ```

14. Keep depth interpolation and depth testing as a separate concern unless a
    failing numerical test proves that they must change with this step.

15. Test on hardware first with cube, then heavytank. Cube acceptance requires
    stable, upright face markings throughout rotation. Heavytank acceptance
    requires its chiral geometry and shared axes texture to remain coherent
    under oblique perspective, where affine or incorrect depth-based UV
    interpolation is most obvious.

16. After hardware passes, test the same canonical binaries in the isolated
    baseline emulator and obtain explicit human validation before committing
    or pushing any emulator-related state.

17. Keep near-plane clipping, texture filtering, lighting, allocation
    validation, and performance optimization outside this UV repair unless a
    test demonstrates a direct blocking dependency.
