# Pingo Terrain development log — 2026-08-02

## Reusable terrain-scene research

The proximate application goal is a scene with an actual ground surface. The
terrain work is deliberately a reusable Pingo facility rather than a special
case for the flight-simulator fixture. Flight makes the requirements obvious:
the ground must give convincing motion, altitude, slope, drainage, vegetation,
settlement, field, road, and runway cues without requiring hand-authored
scenery or an imagery pipeline whose colors collapse badly into Agon's
64-color palette.

The preserved `tests/apps/movefsim` experiment is useful evidence but not a
current implementation template. It established the attractive 320x148 view
over a 320x92 panel, but it is synchronous, moves the camera as the aircraft,
and contains no terrain. Its surviving runway work consists of hand-edited
XCF/PNG/RGBA2222 images, an SVG-to-OBJ experiment, generated runway-number
geometry, and a commented-out legacy model conversion. The SVG converter
turns consecutive path endpoints into ad-hoc triangles without performing
polygon filling, triangulation, winding, topology, or coplanar-overlay
validation. That explains why it could produce geometry without establishing
a robust runway-marking pipeline. These artifacts should remain historical
inputs; they should not constrain the new terrain representation.

### Source-data options

1. **USGS 3DEP DEM as the primary elevation source.** The National Map offers
   seamless 1/3-arc-second (approximately 10 m) and 1-arc-second
   (approximately 30 m) DEMs, as well as higher-resolution 1 m products where
   available. Standard DEMs are Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs, downloads are free
   without an account, and TNMAccess provides an HTTP API for product
   discovery. The 10 m product is the best default source: it preserves far
   more detail than Pingo should normally render, giving the offline compiler
   freedom to filter and decimate rather than forcing it to invent detail.
   The 30 m product is a useful fallback and a natural coarse-LOD reference.
   One-meter DEM and lidar are poor defaults because their acquisition and
   preprocessing costs buy detail that the target renderer and display cannot
   normally show. Sources:
   [USGS elevation formats](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-types-elevation-datasets-are-available-what-formats-do-they-come-and-where-can-i-download),
   [3DEP products](https://www.usgs.gov/3dep-product-news), and
   [TNMAccess API](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/there-api-accessing-national-map-data).

2. **Annual NLCD as the primary color/material key.** Annual NLCD supplies a
   30 m categorical raster with 16 broad land-cover classes, including water,
   developed intensity, barren land, forest types, shrub, grassland, pasture,
   crops, and wetlands. This is much better matched to Agon than aerial
   imagery: each semantic class can map deliberately to one or a small number
   of stable Agon palette colors, while slope, illumination, and restrained
   deterministic variation provide shape. The source is GeoTIFF in a defined
   Albers Equal Area projection. It is currently CONUS-focused; Alaska and
   Hawaii coverage is planned separately, so the compiler must not silently
   imply global coverage. Source:
   [USGS Annual NLCD land cover](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-land-cover-annual-nlcd-collection-1-land-cover).

3. **USDA Cropland Data Layer as an optional rural enhancement.** CDL is a
   crop-specific categorical raster and is now available at 10 m as well as a
   30 m resample. It could distinguish corn, wheat, soy, pasture, and other
   field patterns that NLCD intentionally groups together. This is valuable
   for low-altitude agricultural flight but creates many categories and
   stronger year dependence, so it should refine NLCD agriculture only, not
   become the baseline material source. Source:
   [USDA CDL downloads](https://data.nass.usda.gov/Research_and_Science/Cropland/Release/index.php).

4. **USGS 3DHP/NHD for water geometry.** The evolving 3D Hydrography Program
   provides water bodies and flowlines as downloadable data and web services;
   legacy NHD remains available while elevation-derived 3DHP coverage is
   populated. Polygons are more useful than raster land-cover pixels for
   recognizable lakes and broad rivers. Water can become a small set of flat,
   self-illuminated or simply lit overlay meshes at a controlled elevation,
   while NLCD remains the fallback. Source:
   [USGS 3DHP access](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-hydrography-program/access-3dhp-data-products).

5. **USGS National Transportation Dataset for sparse cultural cues.** The
   downloadable NTD includes roads, railroads, trails, airport complexes,
   airport points, and airport runways in GeoPackage form. Major roads and
   railways can be simplified into sparse colored strips; minor roads should
   be discarded at compilation time. The runway features are a promising
   nationally consistent starting point for airport placement. Source:
   [USGS NTD collection](https://data.usgs.gov/datacatalog/data/USGS%3Aad3d631d-f51f-4b6a-91a3-e617d6a58b4e).

6. **FAA data for airport detail and aviation obstacles.** FAA aeronautical
   open data includes runway geometry and attributes such as designator,
   length, width, and surface type. Its Digital Obstacle File is updated every
   56 days and can later seed towers and other hazards. FAA data is the better
   authority when an airport becomes an interactive destination, while NTD is
   sufficient for initial scenery experiments. Sources:
   [FAA aeronautical data](https://www.faa.gov/data/aero_data),
   [FAA runway data dictionary](https://aeronav.faa.gov/Open_Data_Supp/Data_Dictionary.pdf), and
   [FAA Digital Obstacle File](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/digital_products/dof/).

The National Map is attractive because a single public-domain family already
combines elevation, hydrography, land cover, transportation, structures, and
names. A first compiler can therefore avoid the attribution and distribution
complexity of mixing several community-map licenses while leaving room for
additional sources later. Source:
[The National Map overview](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/national-geospatial-program/national-map).

### Pingo-oriented terrain representation

The recommended base representation is a projected, regular height grid cut
into independently culled square chunks. Each chunk becomes one Pingo mesh and
one object. Neighboring chunks share their complete edge samples so the
highest-detail meshes meet byte-for-byte. The offline compiler records each
chunk's horizontal extent, minimum/maximum height, source provenance, material
classes, and mesh variants in a manifest.

Regular chunks fit the renderer better than one large optimized irregular
mesh:

1. Pingo already performs conservative object-AABB frustum rejection, so a
   chunk wholly outside the view avoids all of its triangle work.
2. A regular grid is deterministic, easy to validate, easy to downsample, and
   gives predictable triangle and storage budgets.
3. Each chunk can have several independently generated discrete LOD meshes.
   This is the useful part of GeoMipMapping without importing a GPU-oriented
   runtime algorithm. The classic technique likewise partitions terrain into
   square patches and reduces patch resolution with distance. Reference:
   [Fast Terrain Rendering Using Geometrical MipMapping](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228494828_Fast_Terrain_Rendering_Using_Geometrical_MipMapping).
4. Whole distant rings can be omitted, while a very coarse horizon belt keeps
   the world from ending visibly.

Geometry clipmaps are intellectually relevant: they use nested regular grids
and a prefiltered elevation pyramid, and were demonstrated for flight over a
large United States height field. Their moving rings, incremental cache, and
seam transitions assume a much more dynamic CPU/GPU geometry path than Pingo
currently exposes. They are therefore a useful model for the offline mip
pyramid and horizon organization, not the first runtime implementation.
Reference:
[Geometry Clipmaps](https://hhoppe.com/geomclipmap.pdf).

An initial compiler should generate at least three variants from one filtered
height source, for example full, half, and quarter sample density. Exact chunk
dimensions, world scale, and switching radii must be selected by a benchmark,
not guessed in this research entry. The benchmark should sweep a low camera
across chunk boundaries and report visible objects, submitted triangles,
object-frustum rejects, renderer time, mesh bytes, and control/PSRAM use.

Current Pingo has no explicit object visibility or deletion subcommand.
However, subcommand 5 (`create object`) establishes an existing object ID and
then republishes its mesh and texture binding while retaining that object's
transformable storage. This suggests that an idle-foreground client can switch
an object's LOD by rebinding the same object ID to a preloaded mesh. This is an
inference from the current implementation, not yet a qualified public client
contract. Before relying on it, add a focused native test and a small visual
fixture proving repeated near/far rebinding, transform preservation, texture
validity, allocation stability, and no render-in-flight mutation. If rebinding
is too costly or semantically awkward, an explicit visibility/mesh-selection
protocol command is the cleaner firmware extension.

### Seams and LOD transitions

Cracks are the principal correctness risk. Independently downsampled adjacent
chunks do not share every edge vertex when their LOD differs. Candidate
solutions, in preferred experiment order, are:

1. **Skirts:** add a narrow downward vertical strip around every chunk. This
   is simple, local, and robust, at the cost of extra triangles and possible
   exposure from very low viewpoints.
2. **Edge-constrained variants:** generate a small finite set of index buffers
   whose north/east/south/west edges collapse to match a coarser neighbor.
   This gives clean surfaces but multiplies mesh variants and runtime state.
3. **Fixed shared edge density:** keep full-resolution boundary vertices while
   simplifying only chunk interiors. This is storage-heavier but removes most
   adjacency combinations and may suit Pingo's small planned scenes.

Skirts are the appropriate first prototype because they isolate chunking and
LOD performance from a more complicated stitching algorithm. They should be
colored from the owning terrain class and extend only enough to cover the
maximum measured cross-LOD gap.

LOD selection should use hysteresis so an aircraft near a threshold does not
rebind a chunk every simulation tick. Changes should be calculated in the
eZ80-owned world state and published only while no render is in flight, just
like current absolute object/camera state. A conservative first policy is
distance bands plus frustum-independent residency: nearby chunks use full
detail, middle chunks use reduced detail, the far belt uses coarse detail, and
beyond the terrain horizon chunks are absent. Later work can add screen-space
error using each chunk's precomputed geometric error.

### Shading without aerial imagery

The baseline should use generated flat-palette shading rather than one giant
texture atlas. Each terrain source cell or simplified triangle receives a
semantic base palette entry from NLCD. Pingo's scene directional light then
provides slope cues. The compiler may add bounded deterministic variations to
large homogeneous regions, but it should avoid per-triangle random noise,
which would shimmer and consume palette contrast without communicating land
form.

A stronger but still tractable scheme combines three offline signals:

1. land-cover class selects the hue family;
2. elevation and/or moisture region makes a small deliberate adjustment; and
3. computed slope/aspect is left primarily to Pingo lighting rather than baked
   twice.

Water, broad roads, and runways should be separate sparse meshes slightly
offset from or conformed to terrain, not painted aerial textures. This keeps
their shapes legible in 64 colors and lets them use independent culling and
LOD. Runway pavement should begin as one rectangle or clipped polygon with
threshold bars, centerline, and designator markings generated from a tiny
purpose-built polygon font. That avoids both the failed SVG path triangulation
and texture quantization. Coplanar z-fighting must be prevented by generating
one controlled surface hierarchy or by conforming overlays with a documented
small vertical offset; arbitrary stacked planes are not acceptable.

### Pipeline options

1. **Offline fixed-region packager — recommended first.** A profile supplies a
   bounding box or airport identifier, target projected coordinate system,
   vertical/horizontal scale, DEM and land-cover product identities, chunk
   policy, LOD policy, palette mapping, and optional vector layers. Python
   tools use GDAL/rasterio-style libraries to crop, reproject, resample,
   filter, triangulate, shade, and emit portable Pingo assembly includes plus
   a machine-readable manifest. All network access is an explicit source-data
   acquisition step; ordinary builds consume tracked or checksum-pinned local
   inputs and remain offline.
2. **Host-side streaming packager.** Generate many scenery chunks but copy
   only a route/region package to SD. This expands available worlds without
   changing the Agon runtime, and is a natural second step.
3. **Runtime SD streaming.** Load/unload terrain around the aircraft during
   flight. This ultimately scales farther, but it introduces file I/O,
   buffering, resource lifetime, UART bandwidth, and asynchronous renderer
   coordination before the basic visual representation is proven. Defer it.
4. **Firmware-resident heightmap terrain renderer.** Upload height/material
   grids and let the VDP select/generate LOD. This could reduce UART geometry
   volume, but creates a second specialized rendering path and substantially
   broadens firmware ownership. It is unjustified before ordinary Pingo
   chunked meshes establish the bottleneck.

### Recommended first experiment

Build one deterministic fixed-region terrain scene around a visually strong
airport with nearby relief, water, forest, developed land, and agriculture.
The scene should initially contain no aircraft physics and no elaborate
runway markings; it exists to qualify terrain.

1. Acquire and checksum-pin USGS 1/3-arc-second DEM, Annual NLCD, and the
   minimum NTD/FAA airport geometry needed for the selected region.
2. Reproject all sources into one local metric tangent plane with the airport
   reference point as a small-coordinate origin. Record horizontal and
   vertical datums and do not treat ellipsoid, orthometric, and model-world
   heights as interchangeable.
3. Generate regular chunks, three discrete LODs, shared high-resolution edge
   samples, and skirts. Emit flat-palette terrain plus separate water and
   runway objects.
4. Create a camera-only asynchronous Pingo fixture that can fly a repeatable
   path at low and medium altitude. Reuse current fixed-step and one-render-in-
   flight architecture.
5. First qualify a static LOD assignment. Then qualify object rebinding and
   distance/hysteresis selection separately.
6. Compare a single-mesh control, chunked single-LOD terrain, and chunked LOD
   terrain using native diagnostics, emulator images, and physical hardware.

The acceptance question is not whether the terrain reproduces a map. It is
whether recognizable landform and categorical surface cues produce convincing
flight motion while Pingo rejects most unavailable work, preserves a stable
horizon and crack-free ground, and stays within measured geometry, PSRAM, and
renderer-time budgets.

This entry records research and a proposed experiment only. No new actionable
work was added to the authoritative TODO, and no implementation is authorized
by this write-up alone.

## Project and emulator setup accepted

The new `pingo_terrain` repository received its standard project scaffold: a
Python 3.14 `.venv`, editable canonical `agonutils`, an editable
`pingo-terrain` package, source-data manifests, profiles, fixtures, tests, and
a reproducible project-local emulator setup script. Downloaded geospatial data,
generated builds, the virtual environment, and the mutable emulator profile
remain ignored.

The current `pingowolf` combined native module passed its complete ordinary
Pingo, Wolf, bridge-robustness, and cross-subsystem smoke suite. The dedicated
profile linked the owned Fab executable, matching Console8 MOS image and map,
and current `vdp_combined.so`; mapped this repository's `fixtures` tree live;
preserved a CRLF `autoexec.txt`; and carried the bespoke marker required for a
fail-closed launch.

The Author interactively reviewed the launched profile and reported that it
was good. The emulator then exited cleanly with status zero. This accepts the
new profile's launch, display, MOS startup, combined-module selection, and
directory-backed SD configuration. No terrain fixture exists yet, so this gate
does not accept terrain rendering behavior.
