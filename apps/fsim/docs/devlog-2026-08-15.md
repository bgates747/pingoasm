## Regular-LOD transition retains coarse fallback

Live travel exposed a lifecycle defect that the cold 227-object Marina proof
could not show: an origin or radius change retired every stale coarse owner in
the rebase transaction, then published missing desired records one per idle
pass. The manager's ordinary convergence loop already had the correct second
half of the policy--desired records are loaded or activated before stale
eviction--but the special rebase path bypassed that ordering and opened a
conspicuous scenery hole.

The regular hierarchy binding now enables a conservative deferred-stale rebase
policy. Rebase repositions every active object into the new floating-origin
frame without changing its active state. The desired plan retains its existing
near-first order, so all missing N=5 cells publish before coarse newcomers.
Only after a full scan proves every desired identity active does ordinary
one-object-per-idle stale eviction begin. Historical regular, natural, and
mixed-distance fixtures explicitly retain the immediate-retirement policy, so
their accepted behavior is unchanged.

Retaining the preceding detailed ring across an adjacent origin requires one
additional cell of placement reach. The regular hierarchy provider's exact
axial translation tables therefore cover `-6..+6` rather than `-5..+5` while
desired near disks remain radius five. This is the proven normal one-cell
handoff fringe, not permission for an arbitrarily delayed multi-cell rebase.

The host transition model confirms that old coarse fallback identities remain
active throughout publication of every adjacent-origin near cell and that the
complete desired ownership is present before stale eviction is modeled. This
policy deliberately prefers temporary overdraw to an empty scene. It is not a
per-coverage-group atomic LOD swap, and live emulator/hardware transition
capture plus Author visual acceptance remain open before commit.

## Regional reflection diagnosed and corrected at ingestion

The Author's Berkeley landmark knowledge exposed a more fundamental defect
than an incorrect initial yaw. A level north-facing Marina view put the East
Bay hills on the left and Bay water on the right; César Chávez Park and the
South Sailing Basin supplied a second local orientation witness. The code
confirmed the cause: the accepted disk payload uses

```
(east, north, up) -> (Pingo X, Y, Z) = (north, up, -east)
```

whose determinant is negative. It is a reflection, not a rotation. Yaw
`-8192` really did point the camera north, but camera-right was Pingo `+Z`,
which the corpus had defined as west. Reversing yaw would merely have hidden
the defect by pointing south.

The regular hierarchy now corrects the complete streamed world at its single
P3DM ingestion boundary. Only after the immutable payload passes structure,
policy, index, and CRC validation, the loader negates every s16 vertex Z and
swaps the second and third position and UV indices of every triangle. Command
52 placement uses `center_east-origin_east`; near q translations and all six
floating-origin steps use the corresponding east-positive sign; the Marina
camera's local Z changes from `466` to `-466`. The resulting published basis
is right-handed `X=north, Y=up, Z=+east`, so north-facing screen-right is east.

This is a compile-time loader policy enabled only by the regular hierarchy
fixture. Historical fixtures compile it out, and no checksum-bound terrain
record is edited or duplicated. The headless cold proof republishes and
traverses all 227 objects, with 34,939 populated terrain pixels and no loader,
CRC, projection, or object-capacity fault. The Author visually accepted the
corrected cardinal arrangement: with the camera facing north, the East Bay
hills are on the right, Bay water is on the left, and the Marina landmarks
again match first-hand geographic knowledge.

## Coverage-group handoff promoted after live travel and radius gates

operation, and exact atomic group commit only for clients that opt into
